The common evaluator, rather than these target generators, supplies execution delay, fees, slippage, fee-inclusive entry limits, funding, terminal liquidation and complete ledgers. All four remain cash before index 90 and produce only `None` or `ETH/USD`. Each signal is a completed-bar decision for a later fill; a recovery trough is not an intraday stop-fill guarantee. None adds to an existing position.

The base entry hurdle used by the two forecast candidates is

`log[(1.008 × 1.0003) / (0.992 × 0.9997)]`.

Its corresponding gross price rise is about **1.674%** before tick effects. This is a cost-derived threshold, not a forecast or assurance that a trade will achieve that move. The indicator and event candidates make no numerical expected-return claim; the evaluator tests whether their realized historical movement is sufficient to pay the same costs. The high round-trip cost makes unnecessary turnover especially consequential.

The Kalman estimator applies established linear state-space inference. Its entry penalty describes uncertainty in the current latent drift under its assumed model. It excludes future process shocks and is not a calibrated loss quantile. The underlying estimation method does not establish an ETH profit opportunity. [Kalman, A New Approach to Linear Filtering and Prediction Problems](https://hans.fugal.net/comps/papers/kalman_1960.pdf).

The recursive regression uses the standard least-squares update with forgetting factor 0.99. Its information matrix and coefficients are updated using each fully observed 14-day outcome. Overlapping outcomes create dependence, so the residual-based mean penalty must not be presented as a frequentist confidence bound. Theory about consistent estimation under suitable excitation and error assumptions does not establish that those assumptions or a profitable relationship hold for BTC and ETH. [Bruce, Goel and Bernstein, Convergence and Consistency of Recursive Least Squares with Variable-Rate Forgetting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.02737).

The volume statistic is a Chaikin-style measure of close location weighted by venue volume. The source describes an interpretation of high-volume closes near a daily high; it does not supply measured ETH profitability. A positive close-location value can occur on a large downward gap, and neither this statistic nor daily native-venue volume is actual signed global order flow. [Chaikin Analytics chart explanation](https://help.chaikinanalytics.com/chaikin-analytics-platform/analytics-chart-overview), [Trading Technologies formula](https://library.tradingtechnologies.com/trade/analytics/charts/technical-indicators/chaikin-money-flow-cmf/).

The recovery rule is an explicitly registered heuristic. The remembered event does not expire while armed, so an old event may remain relevant long after its original peak leaves the trailing window. That is part of its fixed policy, not an unreported adaptive choice. A rebound can fail and a delayed sell can occur below its observed threshold. No source is cited as a theorem supporting its profitability or mathematical originality.
