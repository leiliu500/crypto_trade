For entry fee `fe`, exit fee `fx`, adverse entry slippage `se`, and adverse exit slippage `sx`, the reference-price break-even ratio is `(1+fe)*(1+se)/((1-fx)*(1-sx))`. Fees apply to the executed notional on each leg. With a fee-inclusive budget B and no rounding, a reference-price gross return g gives `net = B * [ (1+g)*(1-fx)*(1-sx)/((1+fe)*(1+se)) - 1 ]`. This is executable-price cost algebra, not a required signal-to-moving-average distance or a forecast of achievable return.

These analytical amounts exclude adverse tick/lot rounding, excess spread or impact, nonfills, and other costs. Maker rows are sensitivities only: changing a fee number does not establish a passive fill, queue priority, or freedom from adverse selection. The stress fee is an experimental penalty, not a claim about a current public fee tier. A 0.10% gross scalp loses about $15.48 under base costs on the first $1,000 budget. Even a 2% gross move loses about $2.20 under stress costs. Frequent turnover repeatedly pays this hurdle. This favors investigating infrequent, sufficiently persistent moves; it does not establish that slow trading is profitable.

The source audit found the following material conventions and limitations:

1. **Funded fee accounting is internally consistent.** Buys round quantity down and include entry fees inside `min($1,000, 10% of cash, cash, 0.1% of past dollar volume)`. Sells credit proceeds less exit fees. Synthetic receipt cash reconstruction and daily P&L reconcile. No additions occur while an unchanged target is held. The cap is 10% of current cash, not 10% of marked equity; marked exposure can grow above the entry cap. Use return on the full $10,000 account as the primary return denominator.
2. **Terminal liquidation is a retrospective convention.** The last close is sold with adverse slippage and fees, and its episode appears in `closedTrades`. Entry count remains a real count of entries, but `closedTrades` cannot be interpreted as naturally completed episodes. Qualification must separately count `exitReason != 'terminal'`; report terminal contribution and remaining-position duration. Do not use an artificial terminal sale as forward proof or force the running ETH40 paper service to match it.
3. **Execution is assumed, especially exits.** `sell()` guarantees the entire position at an OHLC reference, without exit volume, displayed depth, partial-fill, minimum-notional, availability, or rejection checks. A synthetic zero-volume exit still fills fully. Buys also lack historical quotes and order acceptance. The 0.1% daily-volume entry limit cannot establish available depth during a particular opening minute. Rotation sells and buys at the same opening reference with no time between them. This is suitable for cost-aware screening, not an executable-fill claim.
4. **Lag and window boundaries matter.** Base fills at open i+2 and stress at i+3; each evaluation window starts in cash and ignores pre-window target decisions during its first lag days. Apply precisely the same convention to every benchmark. A continuous path is a separate diagnostic, not interchangeable with concatenating cash-reset windows. Both lags size from execution index i−2 volume: this is causal latest-finalized volume, but stress sizing is not based on its older signal candle i−3. Preserve and disclose that convention in this study.
5. **Drawdown names need limits.** Close drawdown uses hypothetical net liquidation marks, including exit costs, each day. Low-price drawdown compares the current day's low mark against the highest previous closing-equity peak; it does not reconstruct an intraday high-to-low path or all missed price events. It is neither a guaranteed realized loss nor a complete intraday maximum.
6. **Input validation belongs outside this small evaluator.** The function assumes aligned, continuous, finite BTC/ETH candles and historically applicable instrument rules; it does not independently enforce all those properties. Freeze source bytes and validate timestamps, alignment, completed-bar availability, prices, volumes, and rules before running candidates. Current tick/minimum values are historical assumptions. Kraken's asset-pair metadata exposes pair-specific precision, minima and status; illustrative documentation values must not replace a captured metadata response. [AssetPairs documentation](https://docs.kraken.com/api-reference/market-data/get-tradable-asset-pairs).

BTC and ETH face the same percentage hurdle under this fixed fee scenario. Daily prices alone do not establish that one has cheaper execution or more executable opportunity. Research BTC and ETH separately using the same funded accounting, matching each asset with its own passive benchmark and cash. Keep symbol-specific quote age, spread, depth, minimum size, volume cap, and rejection records; do not borrow BTC liquidity or metadata for ETH. A larger dollar quote price is not evidence of a larger percentage edge. Neither higher volatility nor a higher historical nominal return is sufficient to select the asset or strategy.

Before any newly selected rule can claim forward support, freeze its equation, inputs, timing, sizing, fee assumption, rejection behavior, and review criterion before observing outcomes. Maintain immutable decision and fill evidence, fresh locally verified books with exchange timestamps, independent account/benchmark receipts, real residual inventory after partial exits, and unavailable marks when quotes fail. Evaluate achieved returns after fees against matched cash/passive exposure, natural completed episodes, concentration in a few episodes, and observed drawdown across the registered review period. A checksum-valid book is evidence of book integrity, not proof that a hypothetical order would fill. Actual execution quality remains unverified by paper trading alone.
