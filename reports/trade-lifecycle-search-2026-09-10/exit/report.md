The exit research freezes three coarse hypotheses for each of BTC/USD and ETH/USD: preserve the thesis, protect a continuing trend with volatility-scaled stops, or exchange some large winners for an asymmetric profit target. None is a profit guarantee or an independently established optimum. The parent study measures each hypothesis together with the entry and holding policies, because an exit changes the next entry opportunity and the number of fees paid.

The current BTC paper runner uses the available delayed weekly trend state. It requests exits on a cash thesis, missing history, or a persistent account drawdown halt, and can trim inventory that exceeds its marked notional cap. Its order flow retries partial exits using fresh books; stale books prevent a simulated fill. The current ETH40 service exits when its completed daily close is at or below SMA40, on its frozen day-i+2 schedule. It has no price stop or fixed profit target; partial residual exits wait for a subsequent eligible cash signal. These details come from [BTC paper logic](../../../src/spot-trend/paper.ts), [ETH40 signal and cycle logic](../../../src/eth40/engine.ts), and [ETH40 execution contract](../../../src/eth40/spec.ts). The common historical experiment does not reproduce every runtime risk or order constraint.

For BTC, the trend hypothesis asks whether a daily volatility barrier improves the downside between relatively slow weekly thesis changes. It can instead stop out a viable weekly trend repeatedly. For ETH40, it asks whether volatility barriers improve on the existing daily SMA exit without truncating the large moves that can support a trend strategy's results. The profit bracket tests a different tradeoff: accepting a capped winner in exchange for earlier realization, with a closer downside trigger. The nominal 2:1 profit/stop distance is not a realized 2:1 payoff once gaps, thesis exits and transaction costs apply.

The precise equations follow the parent [simulator](../simulator.py). Let entry execute at daily open index `e`, with adverse tick-rounded purchase price `P`. At that instant the newest finalized bar is `q=e−2`, for either execution-delay scenario. Define `TR_j = max(high_j−low_j, |high_j−close_(j−1)|, |low_j−close_(j−1)|)` and freeze `A = mean(TR_(q−19), …, TR_q)` at entry. This is a simple mean true range, not Wilder smoothing. Later volatility cannot widen or tighten `A` for that position.

Only finalized post-entry closes `C_t`, with `t≥e`, can trigger risk exits. Set `H_t=max(P,C_e,…,C_t)`. A protective stop triggers when `C_t≤P−sA`; a trailing stop triggers when `C_t≤H_t−rA`; a profit exit triggers when `C_t≥P+pA`. Absent parameters disable their respective barrier. The simulator checks protective stop, trailing stop, then profit exit, followed by maximum holding age and the holding policy's thesis-break logic. Price risk exits override a minimum holding period. Once a sell intent is pending, later observations do not cancel it. A finalized-close signal from bar `t` fills at future open `t+2`, or `t+3` under the delayed scenario, with the declared sell fee and adverse price adjustment. This is not an assumed sale at a touched stop price.

The parent warmup provides sufficient preceding bars for ATR. A missing or zero ATR disables the price barriers rather than inventing a zero-width stop; the parent is recording such entries so they cannot silently qualify as fully protected results. Intraday highs affect true range only after they are historical pre-entry data. Intraday lows or highs after entry do not trigger these close-based exits, and trailing peaks use completed closes only. This convention deliberately leaves the accuracy of exchange-native intraday stops unanswered.

Kraken's native stop order converts a trigger into a market order, incurs taker fees, and may execute materially beyond the stop price. A separate exit also requires cancellation of an independently standing stop where applicable. Those are execution and reconciliation requirements, not evidence that the stop adds expected return. [Kraken stop-loss documentation](https://support.kraken.com/articles/7699391647892-stop-loss-orders).

Native trailing orders follow a favorable reference-price peak and trigger a market order after reversal. The daily research policy instead follows completed closes with an entry-frozen absolute offset. [Kraken trailing-stop documentation](https://support.kraken.com/articles/trailing-stop-orders). Native take-profit orders likewise trigger a market order, so reaching the trigger does not guarantee the intended profit after the actual execution price and fees. [Kraken take-profit documentation](https://support.kraken.com/articles/8407751019284-take-profit-orders).

Optimizing an exit means comparing total net expectancy and downside, not maximizing the win rate. For a fixed set of funded trades, the useful decomposition is `P(win)×mean gross win − P(loss)×mean gross loss − mean round-trip execution costs`; zero-gross trades can still lose their fees. Entry quantity and capital exposure also matter. The exact flat-price round-trip hurdle under 80 bp taker fees and 3 bp adverse price movement on each side is `((1+.008)(1+.0003))/((1−.008)(1−.0003))−1 = 1.673889%`, before tick rounding. The public tier-one spot schedule currently lists 0.80% taker fees; the actual account tier has not been privately verified. [Kraken fee schedule](https://www.kraken.com/features/fee-schedule). A closer stop can improve loss size while making the whole system worse through repeated exit/re-entry costs.

The comparison should inspect net expectancy, closed and sampled downside, natural episode count, fees and turnover, holding duration, rapid re-entries, and the reason for each exit. First compare exit choices with the entry and holding policy fixed; then inspect the full joint combinations. Do not assemble independently attractive entry, hold and exit rows and assume that their gains add. Development-only selection followed by a locked later-period comparison limits new hindsight choices. The existing historical dataset has already been reused, so those later periods are a stress screen, not new independent proof. Forward event-level quotes, intent times, cancellations, partial fills and terminal residuals are necessary to assess native execution behavior.
