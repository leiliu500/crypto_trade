**The four-agent lifecycle search is complete. Neither selected BTC nor ETH combination passed the declared historical screen.** Separating entry firing, order handling, position holding and exits made the causes clearer: entry quality is different from entry frequency, a pending order is different from inventory, and individually attractive components can interact badly.

Profit remains the outcome to measure across the complete process. Each stage needs its own diagnosis, followed by a combined comparison against the current rules, passive exposure and cash. Maximizing firing rate, fill rate, holding duration or winning-trade percentage separately does not establish a better strategy.

Four specialists investigated [entries](entry/report.md), [execution and pending orders](execution/report.md), [position holding](holding/report.md), and [exits](exit/report.md), for BTC and ETH separately. The holding specialist also independently audited the combined experiment. The coordinator implemented a chronological research simulator and tested **27 entry/holding/exit combinations per asset**, with four cost/delay scenarios and four periods: **928 simulation runs including cash and passive controls**. This is a component-interaction experiment, not 54 unrelated mathematical inventions. Maker execution performance remains unranked because the daily data does not contain queue or actual fill evidence.

Each ATR is the simple mean of 20 finalized true ranges, fixed at the actual entry. Price barriers trigger from finalized post-entry closes and execute at a future costed open; they do not imply a fill at a touched stop price. The BTC entry gate requires the existing weekly long regime, signed 28-day displacement/path length of at least 0.35, and a past move above the fixed base cost hurdle. That past displacement is not a forecast of remaining profit. Full formulas, priors and limitations are in the specialist reports and frozen registries.

Candidate definitions, source and data hashes were frozen before this run measured P&L. Selection used January–June 2025 and maximized the worst of four scenario utilities, `net P&L − 0.5 × closing-equity drawdown`. One combination per asset was locked before evaluating later periods. Exact development ties are preserved in [diagnostics.json](diagnostics.json); neither the BTC holding cap nor the ETH thesis-only exit should be presented as empirically superior to its tied alternatives.

The baseline lifecycle normalizes current BTC native-week or ETH40 daily eligibility into this experiment's order-intent, post-entry holding and sizing rules. It is **not an exact replay of either running paper service**. In particular, a pending entry is canceled if its underlying thesis becomes cash before its due open; a one-shot gate need not fire repeatedly. Only finalized bars that began at or after entry count toward holding and exit decisions. These conventions differ from the earlier target-array comparison, so its ETH40 stress result and this baseline's stress result must not be treated as identical experiments. BTC's actual account size, continuous checks, marked-exposure reductions and drawdown halt also differ from the common historical model.

Later windows were tested with separate cash resets. BTC's selected combination earned **−$3.62 and −$52.62** under base assumptions in July–December 2025 and January–September 2026; under combined stress it earned **+$11.12 and −$38.80**. Only one later episode ended naturally. Its positive development return came from a forced boundary liquidation. This does not establish profitable BTC entry/holding rules.

ETH's selected two-bar holding policy stayed profitable in every declared development/later scenario, but failed the episode count, later drawdown limit and consistent improvement over baseline. Its two later base returns were **+$264.12 and +$230.31**, compared with baseline **+$335.75 and +$227.56**. Combined-stress returns were **+$422.17 and +$197.01**, compared with baseline **+$114.68 and +$204.13**. Later natural episodes numbered eight at base timing and seven with delayed timing. Later sampled-low drawdown reached $537.35 at base and $517.52 under combined stress, above the declared $500 limit.

A stress scenario can show a higher return because delayed fills change the entire trading path. It does not mean higher fees improve profit. For ETH, two-bar persistence's continuous improvement over baseline switches from **−$61.82 at base timing** to **+$297.83 with combined cost and delay stress**. That sensitivity prevents a reliable holding-rule upgrade from this sample.

The BTC interaction result directly answers why independently selecting components can mislead. Over the continuous combined-stress replay, adding the selected entry gate alone and the 84-day holding cap alone to baseline suggests a summed **+$33.44** improvement. Applying them together instead changes net by **−$11.17**. The **−$44.61 interaction difference** comes from changed entry/exit timing and subsequent episodes; the component gains do not add. These are post-result diagnostics, not a new selection. [All component removals and interactions](results-v1/summary.json).

The accounting diagnosis is also concrete. BTC's selected base replay generated only **+$6.93 gross reference-price P&L**, then paid **$47.66 fees** and **$1.79 adverse price costs**, leaving **−$42.52**. ETH's selected base replay generated **+$1,060.05 gross**, less **$167.16 fees** and **$6.30 adverse price costs**, leaving **+$886.59**. More BTC entries would need additional gross edge to pay for themselves; cheaper execution alone cannot be assumed to create it. Gross P&L here uses executed quantities and reference prices, so entry costs also affect the funded quantity.

The execution specialist found that current public Tier 1 spot fees are 0.40% maker and 0.80% taker; the actual account tier is unverified. Under the historical base assumptions, the reference-price move needed merely to break even is **1.673889%**, rising to **2.224447%** under cost stress, before extra tick effects. A 1% price gain still loses money in both cases. [Kraken fee schedule](https://www.kraken.com/features/fee-schedule), [reproducible cost calculations](execution/cost-checks-output.json).

The actionable execution reference remains a fresh, price-bounded marketable limit with IOC semantics, fee-inclusive sizing, durable intent and complete fill reconciliation. A lower-fee passive alternative needs a separately defined experiment: for example, one post-only attempt with a 20-second lifetime, then cancellation and reconciliation before any permitted fallback. Twenty seconds is a proposed test duration, not an optimized value. The current BTC/ETH40 no-additions rules also make a fallback after a partial entry a different inventory policy. IOC can leave partial inventory, and a cancellation request does not establish that no fill occurred. [Kraken order semantics](https://docs.kraken.com/api-reference/trading/add-order), [execution review](execution/report.md).

The next implementation should produce one linked record for each eligible opportunity, including the ones that never fill:

1. **Entry decision:** asset, immutable signal ID, completed-bar availability, gate values/rejection reason, intended size and expiry.
2. **Order handling:** decision-time bid/ask and depth, quote timestamps/sequence, intent/request/acknowledgment IDs and times, cancellation/reconciliation status, every partial fill and actual or explicitly assumed fee.
3. **Holding:** actual funded inventory, entry cost, liquidatable valuation, age, thesis state, risk limits and residual quantities.
4. **Exit:** trigger reason/time, submitted quantity, fills, remaining inventory and episode net after both sides' costs.

Use those records to measure net return per eligible opportunity, fill shortfall, adverse selection, turnover, fee burden and drawdown separately for BTC and ETH. Unfilled opportunities remain in the comparison; evaluating only filled winners would bias the result. The present five-minute BTC and roughly 30-second ETH observations cannot resolve a 20-second passive-order path. Event-level public data can support a labeled paper model; actual maker fill-rate claims require actual authorized venue records. This research did not create venue orders.

The existing BTC and ETH40 paper observations should continue with their frozen rules and accounting. No component from this study has qualified for replacement. Any subsequent challenger needs a separate frozen prospective comparison; the reused September 2024–September 2026 history cannot supply an untouched holdout. This study does not change ETH40's registered review requirements, current BTC risk controls, the dashboard port or the disabled distribution model.

The simulator treats future OHLC opens as full fills, uses current instrument rules historically, assumes full exits, and forces terminal liquidation for accounting. Minimum-size exit violations and missing/zero risk ATR are counted and disqualify the screen. Terminal sales pay costs and never count as natural episodes. Daily-low drawdown measures declines from previous observed closing peaks; intraday high-to-subsequent-low drawdown, depth changes, venue cancel races, taxes and infrastructure expense are not reconstructed. Separate-window returns cannot be added and presented as a continuous account.

Validation includes 90 entry causal-prefix checks, 10 independently reviewed synthetic engine groups, 35 exit boundary/gap/causality checks, and reproducible execution accounting. The independent reviewer reconstructed all **928 runs, 6,272 simulated fills and 12,146 events**, including each finalization decision, canceled intent, natural exit and forced terminal sale. Cash, fees, quantities, daily marks, development selection, component interactions and both failed qualification decisions reconcile. No substantive implementation defect was found; numerical residuals were below $0.00000001. All historical exits met the assumed instrument minimums, and every risk-policy entry had positive ATR. Those checks validate the stated simulator, not actual exchange fillability or future profits. See [validation/report.md](validation/report.md) and [validation/audit.json](validation/audit.json).
