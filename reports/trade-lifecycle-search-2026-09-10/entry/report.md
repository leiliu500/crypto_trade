The entry stage now has **three fixed research applications for BTC and three for ETH**: the existing normalized eligibility control plus two gated alternatives for each asset. [Definitions, equations and source hashes](registry.json) were frozen at **2026-09-10 21:55:26 UTC**, before any new lifecycle profit results were inspected. No deployed strategy, account or order route changed.

`build_entries(symbol, bars, baseline_targets)` returns entry permission **while flat**. Every alternative requires the supplied baseline to be eligible; all paths remain false before daily index 90. A false entry gate does not instruct an existing position to sell. The holding and exit specialists determine that behavior separately. The coordinator must apply signal finalization and execution delay once after these completed-bar gates; it must not feed their booleans into an evaluator that interprets every false as a liquidation target.

These are entry applications of established indicators and event logic, not new mathematical inventions. The BTC path ratio is a signed version of Kaufman's displacement-to-traveled-path measure. This gate has a different causal statistic from a moving-average crossing, but its economic thesis remains directional persistence. [TA-Lib's efficiency-ratio documentation](https://ta-lib.org/functions/er.html).

The ETH pressure gate deliberately reuses the previous study's fixed pressure entry equation instead of presenting it as a fresh primitive method. Close-location pressure uses the day's high, low, close and venue volume; it is not observed aggressive buy/sell flow. Its economic interpretation can fail after a gap or when a single venue is unrepresentative. [Chaikin Analytics explanation](https://help.chaikinanalytics.com/chaikin-analytics-platform/analytics-chart-overview), [Trading Technologies formula](https://library.tradingtechnologies.com/trade/analytics/charts/technical-indicators/chaikin-money-flow-cmf/).

The ETH volatility gate uses root-mean-square daily log returns. Multiplying that scale by the square root of five is a fixed descriptive normalization, not a calibrated confidence test or proof of a profitable move. The hypotheses motivating different BTC and ETH gates come from their different frozen baseline horizons and available observations. This study does not establish a universal claim that an asset always needs different mathematics.

The inspected running entry contracts already differ materially. BTC calculates hysteresis from **40 completed native weeks**, entering its long regime above the average times **1.0166**. Its paper runner evaluates throughout the week on nominal five-minute cycles, requires a previously finalized weekly signal, recovers pending orders before planning another one, and permits no additional entry after a first partial fill consumes that week's entry opportunity. ETH40 calculates its long regime from **40 completed daily closes**, using the exact **1.673889%** entry band after 90 warmup bars. Its frozen paper execution opportunity is the first 60 seconds of UTC day `i+2`; missed windows are recorded rather than filled retrospectively, and one fill per account per day is permitted. Both preserve the previous target in their respective hysteresis bands. These facts come from the source files hashed in the registry.

The new gates consume daily observations within those supplied regimes. Adding daily gating to BTC is a new timing experiment around a native-week control. The lifecycle evaluator must identify any normalized sizing, timing or risk assumptions that differ from the running account. Ninety daily bars cannot replace the BTC model's required 40 completed native weeks; its supplied baseline must retain that separate availability constraint.

Firing an entry signal, submitting an order, receiving acceptance and receiving a fill are different events. An eligible bar permits the coordinator to consider a funded order when flat. Submission needs a durable intent identifier, available cash after fees and outstanding reservations, valid minimum size and tick rules, fresh executable prices, and no unresolved prior order. Acceptance proves only that an order was accepted; durable fill receipts and reconciliation establish the acquired quantity. Kraken documents IOC as immediately cancelling whatever quantity cannot fill on arrival, while a matching deadline protects time-sensitive orders from matching after its deadline. These order controls do not establish that a signal is economically favorable. [Kraken Add Order documentation](https://docs-legacy.kraken.com/api/docs/websocket-v2/add_order/).

For both assets, entry diagnostics should separately record eligible opportunities, the gate that blocked each rejected opportunity, submitted requests, acceptance/rejection, partial/full fills, cancellations, quote age and realized shortfall. A pending order belongs to the execution stage; repeated signal evaluation must not create duplicate exposure. A partial fill already creates a holding episode and requires the corresponding asset's no-additions policy. A later failure of these entry gates is not an exit instruction.

A one-shot entry event, such as the BTC pullback crossing, also need not repeat on every intervening bar to keep a finite scheduled intent alive. The coordinator's pre-evaluation refinement cancels a pending entry when its underlying baseline thesis becomes false, while allowing an already-fired gate to disappear before its scheduled fill. The finite due-open time still limits the intent's life. This distinction avoids mechanically rejecting every one-day crossing solely because execution stress adds another day of waiting.

The canonical public Kraken page retrieved on 10 September 2026 shows **0.80% Tier 1 spot taker fees**, while some indexed older URLs still show the superseded 0.40% schedule. The actual account's fee tier remains unverified; simulated account capital does not establish fee eligibility. The common 80-basis-point-per-side fee plus three-basis-point-per-side adverse-price assumption requires a gross reference-price rise of about **1.674%**, before tick effects, merely to break even. The 100/10 stress assumption raises that to about **2.224%**. [Kraken current fee schedule](https://www.kraken.com/features/fee-schedule).

Execution cost checks must use the prospective trade's actual side prices and observed or explicitly assumed fees. If the proposed executed ask is `a` and a future exit bid hypothesis is `b`, its fee-only net cash flow is `q × b × (1 - exitFee) - q × a × (1 + entryFee)`; spread and book-walking effects already reflected in `a` and `b` must not be added a second time. A positive result requires a defensible future-price hypothesis, not simply a distance above a moving average. A fresh quote, a permissive gate, a higher firing rate and a filled order each remain compatible with negative net expectancy.
